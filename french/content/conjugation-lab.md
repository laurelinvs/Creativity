# The Conjugation Lab — 60+ Verb Entries

> *"Les verbes sont les muscles de la langue."*
> — attributed to Victor Hugo (apocryphal, but he might have said it)

Each entry is a verb, but not just a table. It has a translation, a cultural note about when and why to use this particular tense, and an example sentence that sounds like something a real person would say. The verb entries progress from the present tense of fundamental verbs at A1, through the narrative and temporal tenses, to the literary tenses that make a reader feel the weight of history.

## A1 — THE FUNDAMENTAL EIGHT

---

### ÊTRE — to be
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular — the most important French verb

| Person | Présent | Imparfait | Futur | Conditionnel | Passé composé |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| je | suis | étais | serai | serais | été (with avoir) |
| tu | es | étais | seras | serais | — |
| il/elle | est | était | sera | serait | — |
| nous | sommes | étions | serons | serions | — |
| vous | êtes | étiez | serez | seriez | — |
| ils/elles | sont | étaient | seront | seraient | — |

**Note:** *Avoir été* is the passé composé — *être* uses *avoir* as its auxiliary, unlike most movement verbs. *J'ai été* = I was / I have been.

**Cultural note:** *Être* is the verb of identity, and in French it is used where English might use *become* (*il est devenu*), *stand* (*il se trouve que*), or *happen* (*c'est arrivé*). The being of the present — what you are — is one of French philosophy's central obsessions. Sartre's entire philosophy begins with the difference between *être en soi* (being in itself — the brute facticity of things) and *être pour soi* (being for itself — human consciousness). The conjugation of *être* is the philosophy of existence in miniature.

**Example sentence:** *"Je suis où je suis — c'est la vie."* (I am where I am — that's life.)

### AVOIR — to have
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular — the second most important verb

| Person | Présent | Imparfait | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| je | ai | avais | aurai | aurais |
| tu | as | avais | auras | aurais |
| il/elle | a | avait | aura | aurait |
| nous | avons | avions | aurons | aurions |
| vous | avez | aviez | aurez | auriez |
| ils/elles | ont | avaient | auront | auraient |

**Cultural note:** *Avoir raison* (to be right — literally "to have reason"). *Avoir peur* (to be afraid — literally "to have fear"). *Avoir faim* (to be hungry — literally "to have hunger"). French *avoir* is used where English uses *be* for bodily and emotional states — because in French, emotions and sensations are things you *possess*, not states you *inhabit*. This is a philosophical position built into grammar: you are not afraid; you hold fear.

**Example sentence:** *"Il a l'air de quelqu'un qui a beaucoup vécu."* (He has the air of someone who has lived a great deal.)

### ALLER — to go
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular (only *-er* verb with suppletive forms)

| Présent | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|
| je vais | j'irai | j'irais |
| tu vas | tu iras | tu irais |
| il/elle va | il/elle ira | il/elle irait |
| nous allons | nous irons | nous irions |
| vous allez | vous irez | vous iriez |
| ils/elles vont | ils/elles iront | ils/elles iraient |

**Note:** Passé composé uses *être*: *je suis allé(e)*.

**Cultural note:** *Comment ça va ?* — the French greeting formula — is literally "How does it go?" Not "How are you?" but "How is it going?" — the question of movement, progress, process. *Ça va* as the response affirms that the process continues. French greetings are about motion; they ask whether you are still going.

**Example sentence:** *"Ça va aller — j'en suis sûr."* (It'll be fine — I'm sure of it.)

### FAIRE — to make/to do
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular (the third of the great irregular quartet)

| Présent | Futur | Conditionnel | Passé composé |
|---|---|---|---|
| je fais | je ferai | je ferais | j'ai fait |
| tu fais | tu feras | tu ferais | — |
| il/elle fait | il/elle fera | il/elle ferait | — |
| nous faisons | nous ferons | nous ferions | — |
| vous faites | vous ferez | vous feriez | — |
| ils/elles font | ils/elles feront | ils/elles feraient | — |

**Cultural note:** *Faire* is one of French's most productive verbs — it appears in hundreds of expressions: *faire la vaisselle* (do the dishes), *faire confiance* (to trust), *faire semblant* (to pretend), *faire peur* (to frighten), *faire la queue* (to queue). The richness of *faire* constructions is one reason French grammar textbooks seem intimidating — you must learn not just the verb but its idiomatic compounds.

**Example sentence:** *"Qu'est-ce qu'on peut faire ? C'est comme ça."* (What can one do? That's how it is.)

### POUVOIR — to be able to / can
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular modal verb

| Présent | Imparfait | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|---|
| je peux | pouvais | pourrai | pourrais |
| tu peux | pouvais | pourras | pourrais |
| il/elle peut | pouvait | pourra | pourrait |
| nous pouvons | pouvions | pourrons | pourrions |
| vous pouvez | pouviez | pourrez | pourriez |
| ils/elles peuvent | pouvaient | pourront | pourraient |

**Note:** *Puis-je* (May I?) — the inversion form of *je peux* in formal questions. Polite inversion uses the archaic *puis* rather than *peux*: *Puis-je vous aider ?*

**Cultural note:** *Je ne peux plus* — I can't anymore — is one of the most human sentences in French. Exhaustion, saturation, the end of capacity. *Je n'en peux plus* (I can't take it anymore) is the complete phrase. The *en* refers to an unspecified *it* — the weight of things in general.

**Example sentence:** *"Vous pouvez compter sur moi."* (You can count on me.)

### VOULOIR — to want
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular modal verb

| Présent | Imparfait | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|---|
| je veux | voulais | voudrai | voudrais |
| tu veux | voulais | voudras | voudrais |
| il/elle veut | voulait | voudra | voudrait |
| nous voulons | voulions | voudrons | voudrions |
| vous voulez | vouliez | voudrez | voudriez |
| ils/elles veulent | voulaient | voudront | voudraient |

**Note:** *Veuillez* (Please, in formal imperative) — the formal request form derived from *vouloir*: *Veuillez agréer...* in formal letter closings. The formula preserves the old formal imperative.

**Cultural note:** *Je voudrais* (I would like) is the single most important polite formula in French. Not *je veux* (I want — direct, possibly brusque), not *j'aimerais* (I would love — slightly warmer, more personal), but the standard conditional *je voudrais* — the grammar of the polite request. At A1, master *je voudrais* and you have the key to French restaurants, shops, hotels, and offices.

**Example sentence:** *"Je voudrais bien vous dire oui, mais je ne peux pas."* (I'd very much like to say yes, but I can't.)

### SAVOIR — to know (a fact, how to do something)
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular

| Présent | Futur | Conditionnel | Subjonctif |
|---|---|---|---|
| je sais | saurai | saurais | que je sache |
| tu sais | sauras | saurais | que tu saches |
| il/elle sait | saura | saurait | qu'il sache |
| nous savons | saurons | saurions | que nous sachions |
| vous savez | saurez | sauriez | que vous sachiez |
| ils/elles savent | sauront | sauraient | qu'ils sachent |

**Cultural note:** *Je ne sais pas* — "I don't know" — is, according to linguists, one of the most spoken sentences in the history of any language. In French, the Socratic tradition valorizes *je ne sais pas* as a philosophical position: to acknowledge ignorance is the beginning of wisdom. Voltaire's relentless *je ne sais pas ce que c'est* (I don't know what this is) — applied to God, the soul, the universe — was not evasion but honest inquiry.

**Example sentence:** *"Tu sais quoi — laisse tomber."* (You know what — forget it.)

### CONNAÎTRE — to know (a person, a place)
**Level:** A1 | **Type:** Irregular (*-aître* conjugation)

| Présent | Passé composé | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|---|
| je connais | j'ai connu | connaîtrai | connaîtrais |
| tu connais | — | — | — |
| il/elle connaît | — | — | — |
| nous connaissons | — | — | — |
| vous connaissez | — | — | — |
| ils/elles connaissent | — | — | — |

**Note:** The circumflex on *connaît* disappears in the 2021 spelling reform; both spellings are currently accepted.

**Cultural note:** The *savoir/connaître* distinction is one of French grammar's most culturally revealing. *Savoir* is propositional knowledge — facts, skills, information. *Connaître* is acquaintance — relational, personal knowledge. *Je sais que Paris est en France* (I know that Paris is in France — fact). *Je connais Paris* (I know Paris — I have experienced it personally). This distinction, absent in English ("I know"), is present in German (*wissen/kennen*), Italian (*sapere/conoscere*), and Spanish (*saber/conocer*). It suggests that these language families inherited a philosophical distinction from Latin (*scire* vs *noscere*) that the English tradition lost.

**Example sentence:** *"Je la connais depuis toujours — on a grandi ensemble."* (I've known her forever — we grew up together.)

**Level:** A2 | **Type:** Regular *-er* reflexive; *e → è* spelling change before mute syllable

| Présent | Passé composé | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|---|
| je me lève | je me suis levé(e) | je me lèverai | je me lèverais |
| tu te lèves | — | — | — |
| il/elle se lève | — | — | — |
| nous nous levons | — | — | — |
| vous vous levez | — | — | — |
| ils/elles se lèvent | — | — | — |

**Note:** Passé composé uses *être*; past participle agrees with subject: *elle s'est levée*.

**Cultural note:** The morning ritual (*la routine matinale*) in French is expressed almost entirely through reflexive verbs: *se réveiller, se lever, se doucher, se raser, se coiffer, se brosser les dents, s'habiller, se regarder dans la glace, se préparer*. ### VENIR — to come
**Level:** A2 | **Type:** Irregular (*-enir* group)

| Présent | Futur | Conditionnel | Passé composé |
|---|---|---|---|
| je viens | viendrai | viendrais | je suis venu(e) |
| tu viens | viendras | viendrais | — |

**Note:** *Venir de + infinitive* = recent past: *Je viens de manger* (I just ate — I have just eaten). This is the most elegant way to express the very recent past in French.

Waiting for someone to come is one of the most fundamentally human activities; French grammar gives it a dedicated construction (*venir de* for just-past, the future simple for expected arrival).

### VIVRE — to live
**Level:** B1 | **Type:** Irregular (*-vre* group)

| Présent | Imparfait | Passé composé | Futur | Passé simple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| je vis | vivais | j'ai vécu | vivrai | je vécus |
| tu vis | vivais | — | — | — |
| il/elle vit | vivait | — | — | — |
| nous vivons | vivions | — | — | — |
| vous vivez | viviez | — | — | — |
| ils/elles vivent | vivaient | — | — | — |

**Note:** *Vivre* (to live a life) vs *habiter* (to reside in a place). *Je vis à Paris* and *j'habite à Paris* both mean "I live in Paris" — but *je vis à Paris* suggests a fuller presence, a life lived there; *j'habite* is purely residential.

**Cultural note:** *La vie est ailleurs* — life is elsewhere — the title of Milan Kundera's 1973 novel (written in French during his Czech exile). The French phrase for the Romantic desire that the real life is always somewhere else. To *vivre* is to be fully present; the feeling that one is not yet fully living — that the real *vie* is elsewhere — is one of French literature's permanent themes, from Emma Bovary to Rimbaud's *Une Saison en Enfer*.

**Example sentence:** *"Il faut bien vivre — et pour ça, il faut travailler."* (You have to live — and for that, you have to work.)

Belief is belief, whether its object is human or divine.

### SAVOIR vs POUVOIR in Ability

**Level:** B1 | **Type:** Conceptual entry — how modals create meaning

*Savoir + infinitive* = ability through learning/skill: *Je sais nager.* (I know how to swim — I learned.)
*Pouvoir + infinitive* = ability through circumstance/permission: *Je peux nager.* (I can swim — I'm allowed to, or I'm in a condition to.)

The subtle difference: *Il sait cuisiner mais ce soir il ne peut pas — il est trop fatigué.* (He knows how to cook but tonight he can't — he's too tired.) The competence is permanent (*savoir*); the current circumstance is temporary (*pouvoir*).

**Cultural note:** Descartes' *cogito* uses *je pense* (I think) — not *je sais que je pense* (I know that I think). The immediate act of thinking is not presented as *savoir* (a skill) but as a present tense certainty. The philosophy of knowledge (*épistémologie*) in French distinguishes heavily between different kinds of knowing — a legacy of both Descartes' radical doubt and of the French educational tradition that drills epistemology into lycéens.

## C1 — ARGUMENTATION AND NUANCE

---

### PRÉTENDRE — to claim/maintain (not "to pretend")
**Level:** C1 | **Type:** Regular *-re* | **False friend warning**

| Présent | Passé composé | Futur | Conditionnel |
|---|---|---|---|
| je prétends | j'ai prétendu | prétendrai | prétendrais |
| tu prétends | — | — | — |
| il/elle prétend | — | — | — |
| nous prétendons | — | — | — |
| vous prétendez | — | — | — |
| ils/elles prétendent | — | — | — |

**Critical note:** *Prétendre* in French = to claim, to assert (often with a hint of doubt about the claim's truth). It does NOT mean "to pretend" in English. English "to pretend" = *faire semblant de*.

*Il prétend être médecin.* = He claims to be a doctor. (I doubt it.)
*Il fait semblant d'être médecin.* = He pretends to be a doctor. (He's acting.)

**Cultural note:** *Prétendre* carries the implicit skepticism of the French critical mind. When a French text says *il prétend que*, the reader automatically understands that the narrator is not endorsing the claim. This is one of French's built-in evidentiality markers — the grammar of epistemic distance.

| il/elle perçoit | — | — | — |
| nous percevons | — | — | — |
| vous percevez | — | — | — |
| ils/elles perçoivent | — | — | — |

**Note:** Also: *décevoir* (to disappoint — same conjugation), *recevoir* (to receive), *concevoir* (to conceive).

**Cultural note:** The French philosophical tradition distinguishes between *percevoir* (the sensory/cognitive act of perception) and *concevoir* (the intellectual act of conception). Descartes' *Méditations* turn on this: the senses deceive (*percevoir* is unreliable); the intellect is more trustworthy. The *cogito* survives because it is an act of *concevoir*, not *percevoir*.

### DISCOURIR — to discourse/hold forth
**Level:** C1 | **Type:** Irregular (*-ir* verb, rare)

*Discourir* = to speak at length, to hold forth, to lecture. Used to describe the French intellectual habit of extended oral argument.

*Il a discouru pendant une heure sur la réforme.* (He held forth for an hour about the reform.)

**Cultural note:** The *grand discours* (great speech) is a French institution — de Gaulle's speeches, Jaurès's Parliamentary addresses, Blum's speeches to the Chamber. The length and substance of political oratory in France is extraordinary by comparative standards. A political speech that is not a *discours* — that does not argue, develop, and conclude — is considered insufficient. The capacity to *discourir* coherently is the mark of the educated French citizen.

## C2 — LITERARY AND HISTORICAL TENSES

---

### ÊTRE — Subjonctif Imparfait (Literary)

**Level:** C2 | The literary subjunctive of existence

| Person | Subjonctif Imparfait |
|---|---|
| je | que je fusse |
| tu | que tu fusses |
| il/elle | qu'il fût |
| nous | que nous fussions |
| vous | que vous fussiez |
| ils/elles | qu'ils fussent |

**Example:** *Il était impossible qu'il **fût** encore en vie.* (It was impossible that he should still be alive.)
Modern equivalent: *Il était impossible qu'il **soit** encore en vie.*

**Cultural note:** The subjonctif imparfait of *être* — *qu'il fût* — is among the most beautiful sounds in literary French. The grave accent on the circumflex, the rounded vowel, the distance it creates from the present — it is the grammatical sound of a world definitively past. Flaubert used it; Stendhal used it; Yourcenar used it. When you encounter it in reading, you are reading literature that takes its own language seriously.

---

### VOIR — Passé Simple
**Level:** C2 | The narrative past of sight and insight

| Person | Passé Simple |
|---|---|
| je | vis |
| tu | vis |
| il/elle | vit |
| nous | vîmes |
| vous | vîtes |
| ils/elles | virent |

**Example:** *Il vit la lumière au bout du couloir et s'y dirigea sans hésiter.* (He saw the light at the end of the corridor and made for it without hesitating.)

**Cultural note:** *Vit* (passé simple of *voir* — he saw) looks identical to *vit* (present of *vivre* — he lives). Context distinguishes them in narrative: *il vécut* (passé simple of *vivre*) vs *il vit* (présent of *vivre*). The passé simple of *voir* appearing in literary narrative is one of the small tests of the reader who has truly arrived. *Il vit.* He saw. Everything that follows is a consequence.

---

### RENDRE — and the *Rendre + adj* Construction
**Level:** C2 | **Type:** Regular *-re*

*Rendre + adjective* = to make something become: *rendre quelqu'un heureux* (to make someone happy), *rendre la chose impossible* (to make the thing impossible), *rendre service* (to do a service, to help).

**Cultural note:** *Rendre l'âme* (to give back the soul — to die). *Rendre les armes* (to lay down arms). *Se rendre* (to surrender, to go: *je me rends à Paris*). *Rendre à César ce qui appartient à César* (render unto Caesar). The verb *rendre* — to give back, to return, to make — is one of the verbs that reveals French grammar's Latin architecture most clearly. *Rendre* comes from Latin *reddere* — to give back — and it carries both the accounting logic of Latin and the existential resonance of what is owed and what must be returned.
